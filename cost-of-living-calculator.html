Cost of Living Calculator

What This Calculator Estimates

Cost of living comparisons estimate how far your income or budget would go in a different city or country by adjusting for relative price differences in housing, groceries, transportation, and other everyday expenses, though actual comparisons depend heavily on your specific lifestyle and spending habits rather than a single universal index. This calculator provides a general adjustment estimate using the index or ratio figures you provide for your specific comparison.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your current monthly living cost.
  2. Enter the target monthly living cost you want to compare.
  3. Click Compare Cost of Living.
  4. Review the monthly and annual difference.
  5. Update the amounts when your assumptions change.

Tips for a More Accurate Estimate

  • Cross-check cost of living figures across a few different sources, since they can vary.
  • Remember housing is often the biggest driver of cost of living differences between cities.
  • Factor in salary differences separately, since this tool only adjusts for cost, not income.
  • Consider your specific lifestyle, since generic indices may not reflect your actual spending pattern.
  • Use this as a starting point for relocation planning, not a precise budgeting tool.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a cost of living index actually measure?

It's typically a relative comparison of prices for a basket of common goods and services (housing, food, transport, utilities) between two locations, expressed as an index or percentage difference.

Why do cost of living comparisons vary between different sources?

Different data providers weight categories differently and sample different goods/services, so index numbers for the same city pair can vary — treat any single source as an estimate, not an exact figure.

Does this account for salary differences in different job markets?

No, this estimates how your current budget or salary would need to adjust for price differences — it doesn't account for the fact that salaries themselves often differ significantly between locations too.

Is housing weighted the same as other expenses in most indices?

Housing is often the largest and most variable component of cost of living differences between cities, so many indices weight it heavily — check your source's specific methodology.

Where can I find reliable cost of living data for specific cities?

Several independent cost of living index providers and government statistical agencies publish comparative data — cross-check a few sources for the most reliable estimate.

This calculator provides cost-of-living estimates only. Actual housing, tax, transport, and lifestyle costs can vary, so use the result as a planning estimate only.
