Salary Calculator UK

Computes real 2026/27 Income Tax bands and National Insurance automatically for England, Wales & Northern Ireland — just enter your gross salary and optional pension rate. (Scotland uses separate tax bands not covered here.)

What This Calculator Estimates

UK salaries are subject to Income Tax and National Insurance contributions, both administered by HMRC. This calculator computes your exact Income Tax using the current 2026/27 bands and Personal Allowance (including the taper above £100,000), plus National Insurance, automatically from your gross salary — covering England, Wales, and Northern Ireland. Scotland uses a separate 6-band system not covered here.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your annual gross salary in pounds.
  2. Optionally adjust the pension contribution rate.
  3. Click Calculate UK Take-Home Pay.
  4. Review your exact Income Tax, National Insurance, and net pay.

Worked Example

On a £40,000 annual salary: Personal Allowance is the full £12,570 (no taper below £100,000), leaving £27,430 taxable, all within the 20% basic-rate band — Income Tax = £5,486. National Insurance = (£40,000 − £12,570) × 8% = £2,194.40. With a 5% pension contribution (£2,000), net take-home pay is £30,319.60/year (£2,526.63/month).

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the main deductions from a UK salary?

Income Tax (progressive bands via HMRC) and National Insurance contributions are the two main statutory deductions, alongside pension contributions where applicable under auto-enrolment.

Does this calculator compute exact Income Tax automatically?

Yes, for England, Wales, and Northern Ireland. It applies the current 2026/27 bands and your tapered Personal Allowance automatically from your gross salary. Scotland uses separate bands not covered here.

Is National Insurance the same as Income Tax?

No, National Insurance is a separate contribution funding state benefits and pension entitlement, calculated using its own thresholds distinct from Income Tax bands.

What is a tax code and how does it affect take-home pay?

Your tax code, assigned by HMRC, determines how much tax-free personal allowance you receive before Income Tax applies — an incorrect tax code can significantly affect your net pay.

Does auto-enrolment pension reduce my take-home salary?

Yes, most UK employees are automatically enrolled into a workplace pension, with a percentage of salary deducted unless you opt out, which reduces take-home pay but builds retirement savings.
