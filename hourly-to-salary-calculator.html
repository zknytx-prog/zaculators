Frequently Asked Questions

What's the standard formula for converting hourly to annual salary?

The common baseline is hourly rate × 40 hours/week × 52 weeks/year (2,080 hours), but this assumes full-time, no unpaid leave — adjust for your actual schedule for accuracy.

How does overtime affect this conversion?

If you regularly work overtime at a premium rate, a simple hourly-to-annual conversion using standard hours will understate your actual annual earnings — factor in overtime hours and rate separately if relevant.

What if I take significant unpaid time off during the year?

Reduce your total annual hours to reflect actual worked weeks if you take substantial unpaid leave, since this calculator's default assumes continuous full-time work.

Is this the same as a gross-to-net salary calculator?

No, this estimates your gross annual salary equivalent from an hourly rate. It doesn't account for taxes or other deductions that would apply once you have that annual figure.

Why do employers sometimes quote hourly and sometimes annual salary?

This varies by industry and role type — hourly is common for part-time, contract, or non-exempt positions, while annual salary is typical for full-time exempt roles, though both represent the same underlying compensation once converted.

This calculator provides salary estimates only. Actual gross and net pay can vary by schedule, overtime, benefits, and local rules.
