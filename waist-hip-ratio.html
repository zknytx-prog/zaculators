What This Calculator Estimates

Waist-to-hip ratio compares waist circumference to hip circumference, used as an indicator of body fat distribution and associated health risk, since carrying more weight around the waist (an 'apple' shape) is generally associated with different health risk patterns than carrying weight around the hips (a 'pear' shape). This simple ratio is often used alongside BMI to give additional context about body composition and fat distribution.

Formula / Method Used

  1. Measure your waist circumference at the narrowest point or just above the navel, depending on your tracking method.
  2. Measure your hips at the widest point.
  3. Enter both measurements in the same unit.
  4. The calculator uses waist / hip and then compares the ratio with a simple threshold: 0.90 for male and 0.85 for female entries on this page.

Worked Example

If your waist is 85 cm and your hips are 95 cm, your ratio is 85 / 95 = 0.89. On this page, a female entry at 0.89 would be labeled in the higher-risk range, while a male entry at 0.89 would be labeled in the lower-risk range.

What the Result Means

The ratio helps describe where body mass is carried. Higher ratios can suggest more weight carried around the waist. This is a screening signal only and should be interpreted with context such as BMI, medical history, and professional guidance.

Common Mistakes

  1. Mixing inches for one measurement and centimeters for the other.
  2. Measuring the waist or hips at inconsistent points each time.
  3. Pulling the tape too tight or measuring over bulky clothing.
  4. Treating the ratio as a diagnosis instead of a screening tool.

Tips for a More Accurate Estimate

  • Measure at the narrowest waist point and widest hip point for consistency.
  • Take measurements at the same time of day for the most comparable results over time.
  • Use this alongside BMI and body fat percentage for a fuller picture, not as a standalone metric.
  • Track trends over time rather than fixating on a single measurement.
  • Discuss any health concerns related to this ratio with a healthcare provider.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does waist-to-hip ratio actually measure?

It's the ratio of your waist circumference to your hip circumference, used as an indicator of how body fat is distributed, which relates to certain health risk patterns.

Why does fat distribution matter, not just total body fat?

Research generally associates central/abdominal fat distribution with different health risk profiles compared to fat carried in the hips and thighs, making distribution a useful additional data point beyond total body fat or weight.

How should I measure my waist and hip circumference accurately?

Measure your waist at the narrowest point (typically around the belly button area) and hips at the widest point, using a flexible tape measure without pulling too tight, ideally at the same time of day for consistency.

Is waist-to-hip ratio better than BMI?

They measure different things — BMI relates weight to height, while waist-to-hip ratio reflects fat distribution. Many health professionals use both together rather than favoring one over the other exclusively.

Can waist-to-hip ratio change without changing body weight?

Yes, since it reflects distribution rather than total mass, body composition changes (like fat loss with muscle gain) can shift this ratio even if total weight stays similar.

Limitations / Disclaimer

This calculator provides a simple screening estimate only. It does not account for age, ethnicity, pregnancy, athletic build, body composition testing, or medical history. It is not medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment guidance.
