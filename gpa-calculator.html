GPA Calculator

What This Calculator Estimates

GPA (Grade Point Average) is calculated by converting letter grades to point values (commonly on a 4.0 scale), multiplying each by the course's credit hours, summing these weighted points, and dividing by total credit hours attempted. This calculator estimates your GPA using the grades and credit hours you provide, useful for tracking academic progress across a semester or cumulative enrollment.

Formula / Method Used

GPA = Σ(Grade Points × Credit Hours) ÷ Σ(Credit Hours), summed across all courses entered.

Worked Example

Courses with grade points 4.0 (3 credits), 3.0 (3 credits), and 3.7 (4 credits) give a weighted total of 12 + 9 + 14.8 = 35.8, divided by 10 total credits, for a GPA of 3.58.

How to Interpret the Result

This GPA reflects only the courses entered. For a full cumulative GPA, include every course from every term you want counted.

Common Mistakes

  • Using letter grades directly instead of converting to grade points first.
  • Forgetting to weight by credit hours, which skews the average.
  • Leaving out repeated courses your school counts differently.
  • Mixing standard and weighted (honors/AP) scales inconsistently.

Tips for a More Accurate Estimate

  • Confirm your school's specific GPA scale and point value assignments before calculating.
  • Include accurate credit hours for each course, since these weight the GPA calculation.
  • Distinguish between semester and cumulative GPA depending on what you're trying to track.
  • Recalculate each semester as new grades are finalized to track your academic progress.
  • Use this to model what grades you'd need in future courses to reach a target GPA.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is GPA calculated from letter grades?

Each letter grade is assigned a point value (commonly A=4.0, B=3.0, C=2.0, etc.), multiplied by the course's credit hours, then all these weighted points are summed and divided by total credit hours to get the GPA.

Why do credit hours matter for GPA calculation?

Courses with more credit hours count more heavily toward your overall GPA, reflecting their greater weight in your academic course load compared to lower-credit courses.

What's the difference between semester GPA and cumulative GPA?

Semester GPA reflects only courses taken in a specific term, while cumulative GPA reflects your entire academic history to date — both are commonly tracked for different purposes.

Do all schools use the same 4.0 GPA scale?

No, while a 4.0 scale is common, some institutions use different scales (like a 5.0 scale for honors/AP courses) or different point value assignments — check your specific school's grading policy.

How can I estimate what GPA I need going forward to reach a target?

Using your current cumulative GPA and credit hours alongside planned future courses and expected grades, you can back-calculate what average you'd need in remaining courses to reach a target cumulative GPA.
