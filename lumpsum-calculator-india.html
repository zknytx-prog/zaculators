Frequently Asked Questions

How does lumpsum investing differ from a SIP?

Lumpsum involves investing a single amount all at once, exposing your full investment to market conditions immediately, while a SIP spreads contributions over time, averaging your purchase cost through rupee-cost averaging.

Is lumpsum or SIP investing better?

This depends on market timing, your risk tolerance, and available capital — lumpsum can outperform in consistently rising markets, while SIP can help manage volatility risk, so the better choice depends on individual circumstances and market conditions.

What return rate should I use for a lumpsum estimate?

Use a realistic assumption based on the underlying fund's asset class and historical performance range, being conservative since past returns don't guarantee future results.

Does lumpsum investing carry more risk than SIP?

Generally yes, since your full investment is exposed to market conditions at a single point in time, whereas SIP spreads that risk across multiple entry points over time.

Can I combine lumpsum and SIP investing strategies?

Yes, many investors use both approaches — for example, investing a lumpsum initially and then continuing with regular SIP contributions, or vice versa, depending on available capital and market conditions.
