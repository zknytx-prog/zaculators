What This Calculator Estimates

US state income tax varies enormously by state — some states (like Texas and Florida) levy no state income tax at all, others use a flat rate, and many use progressive brackets similar to the federal system but with entirely different thresholds and rates. Frequently Asked Questions

Do all US states have income tax?

No, several states (such as Texas, Florida, Nevada, and others) levy no state income tax at all, while the rest use either a flat rate or a progressive bracket system.

Is state income tax calculated the same way as federal tax?

Not necessarily — some states use flat rates, others use progressive brackets with their own thresholds, and rules on deductions and credits vary significantly by state.

Can I owe state tax in a state I don't live in?

Sometimes, particularly if you earn income in a different state than your residence — many states have reciprocity agreements or specific nonresident tax rules, so check your specific situation.

Does this calculator know my state's specific rules automatically?

No, since state rules vary enormously, you need to enter the rate applicable to your specific state and income level manually.

Where can I confirm my state's current tax rules?

Your state's department of revenue or taxation website publishes current rate tables, brackets, and filing requirements.
