What This Calculator Estimates

Daily calorie needs depend on your Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) plus your activity level, typically calculated by multiplying BMR by an activity multiplier ranging from sedentary to very active, giving an estimate of Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) — the calories needed to maintain your current weight. From there, calorie needs for weight loss or gain are typically calculated as a deficit or surplus from this maintenance baseline.

Tips for a More Accurate Estimate

  • Choose an activity multiplier that reflects your typical week, not just your best days.
  • Remember this gives a maintenance estimate — weight loss/gain requires a deficit or surplus from that baseline.
  • Recalculate as your weight or activity level changes significantly over time.
  • Pair calorie needs with adequate protein intake if your goal includes muscle maintenance or growth.
  • Treat this as a starting estimate to adjust based on real-world results, not a fixed prescription.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is daily calorie need calculated?
It's typically your BMR multiplied by an activity factor reflecting how active you are, resulting in an estimated maintenance calorie level (TDEE).

What activity multiplier should I use?
Common ranges go from sedentary to very active — choose the level that best reflects your typical week, not just your best or worst days.

How many calories should I eat to lose weight?
Weight loss typically requires eating below your maintenance calorie level, though the appropriate deficit depends on your goals and individual factors.

Does this calculator account for specific dietary goals like muscle gain?
No, this estimates general calorie needs. Specific goals involve additional considerations like protein intake and training program.

How often should I recalculate my calorie needs?
Recalculate whenever your weight, activity level, or goals change significantly.
