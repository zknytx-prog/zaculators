Grade Converter

What This Calculator Estimates

Grade conversion translates between different grading scales (like percentage, letter grades, or GPA points), which vary by country, institution, and grading system, making direct comparison of grades from different systems require a clear conversion reference. This calculator converts between grading scales using the value and systems you specify, useful for students dealing with transcripts or applications across different educational systems.

Formula / Method Used

Standard scale used: 97-100=A+ (4.0), 93-96=A (4.0), 90-92=A- (3.7), 87-89=B+ (3.3), 83-86=B (3.0), 80-82=B- (2.7), 77-79=C+ (2.3), 73-76=C (2.0), 70-72=C- (1.7), 67-69=D+ (1.3), 60-66=D (1.0), below 60=F (0.0).

Worked Example

An 88% score converts to a B+ letter grade, equal to 3.3 grade points on the standard 4.0 scale.

How to Interpret the Result

Use the letter grade and grade points as an estimate — always check your syllabus, since some schools use different cutoffs or skip plus/minus grading entirely.

Common Mistakes

  • Assuming every school uses identical percentage cutoffs.
  • Ignoring rounding rules your instructor may apply (e.g. 89.5% rounding up to 90%).
  • Using this for pass/fail or non-traditional grading systems where it doesn't apply.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do grading scales differ so much between countries and institutions?

Educational systems developed independently across different countries and even individual institutions, leading to varied grading scales (percentage-based, letter grades, GPA point systems, or other numeric scales) that don't always translate directly.

Is there a universal standard for converting between grading scales?

No single universal standard exists — conversion often depends on the specific institutions or countries involved, and some conversions require approximation since scales don't always align perfectly.

How is GPA typically calculated from letter grades?

GPA is commonly calculated by assigning point values to letter grades (like A=4.0, B=3.0) and averaging these across courses, sometimes weighted by credit hours — specific scales can vary by institution.

Should I use this for official transcript conversions?

This provides a general estimate; official transcript evaluations for academic or immigration purposes typically require using a recognized credential evaluation service rather than an informal calculator.

Why might my converted grade look different from what my school reports?

Institutions may use slightly different conversion tables or rounding rules, so always confirm with your specific school's official grading policy for the most accurate conversion.
