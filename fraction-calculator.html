Frequently Asked Questions

Why do fractions need a common denominator for addition and subtraction?

Fractions represent parts of a whole, and you can only directly combine parts that are divided into the same number of pieces (the denominator), which is why finding a common denominator is necessary before adding or subtracting.

Is finding a common denominator necessary for multiplying fractions?

No, multiplying fractions is more direct — you simply multiply the numerators together and the denominators together, without needing a common denominator first.

How does dividing fractions work?

Dividing by a fraction is equivalent to multiplying by its reciprocal (flipping the numerator and denominator), which is why 'invert and multiply' is a common shortcut taught for fraction division.

Why does this calculator simplify the result?

Simplifying to lowest terms (dividing both numerator and denominator by their greatest common divisor) gives the cleanest, most standard representation of the answer.

Can this calculator handle mixed numbers?

Depending on the specific inputs, mixed numbers typically need to be converted to improper fractions first before performing arithmetic, then converted back if a mixed number result is preferred.
