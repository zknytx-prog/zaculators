Frequently Asked Questions

How is a ratio simplified to its lowest terms?

A ratio is simplified by dividing all parts by their greatest common divisor (GCD), resulting in the smallest whole numbers that maintain the same proportional relationship — for example, 4:8 simplifies to 1:2.

What's the difference between a ratio and a fraction?

A ratio compares two or more quantities to each other, while a fraction represents a part relative to a whole — related concepts, but ratios are often used specifically for comparing separate quantities rather than parts of one total.

Can a ratio have more than two terms?

Yes, ratios can compare three or more quantities (like 2:3:5), following the same simplification principle of dividing all terms by their greatest common factor.

How do I find an equivalent ratio?

Multiply or divide all terms of a ratio by the same number to get an equivalent ratio that maintains the same proportional relationship, just expressed with different numbers.

Where are ratios commonly used in everyday life?

Common uses include recipe scaling, mixing solutions or paint colors, financial ratios in business analysis, map scales, and aspect ratios in photography and video.
