How this estimate works

This uses a simplified base fee plus a per-pound rate to give you a rough planning number — real carrier rates depend on exact dimensions, dimensional weight, carrier, service speed, fuel surcharges, and destination country, which can swing the actual price significantly in either direction.

Use this for planning, not quoting customers

Before you commit to a shipping price on a listing or invoice, get an actual quote from USPS, UPS, FedEx, or DHL for your exact package — this tool is meant to help you sanity-check pricing decisions, not replace a real rate lookup.

Dimensional weight can catch you off guard

Large, lightweight packages are often charged by dimensional weight (size) rather than actual weight — a big box of pillows can cost as much to ship as a small box of dumbbells. Factor this in for bulky, low-density products.

FAQ

Why is this only an estimate and not exact rates? Real shipping costs depend on precise dimensions, carrier-specific pricing, fuel surcharges, and delivery speed — accurately replicating every carrier's live rate table isn't something a simple calculator can do reliably. Always confirm with your carrier before finalizing pricing.

What counts as 'nearby' vs 'far' international? Nearby regions are generally neighboring countries or short-haul international routes; far regions are longer-haul, cross-continental shipments, which typically cost meaningfully more per pound.

Should I build shipping cost into my product price instead? Many sellers do — offering 'free shipping' with the cost baked into the item price tends to convert better than showing a separate shipping charge, even though the buyer pays the same total either way.
