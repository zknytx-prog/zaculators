VAT Calculator UAE

What This Calculator Estimates

Value Added Tax (VAT) in the UAE is a consumption tax administered by the Federal Tax Authority (FTA), applied at a flat statutory rate on most goods and services, with certain categories zero-rated or exempt. Because VAT is a single flat percentage rather than a bracketed system, this calculator multiplies your entered amount by the rate you provide — keep the rate current with FTA's official published rate since it's a single figure applied uniformly rather than a progressive scale.

Tips for a More Accurate Estimate

  • Confirm the current standard VAT rate with the Federal Tax Authority (FTA) before using this for invoicing.
  • Check whether your specific goods or services are zero-rated or VAT-exempt under FTA rules.
  • Keep VAT collected and VAT paid records separate for accurate return filing.
  • Registered businesses should file VAT returns on FTA's required schedule, typically quarterly or monthly depending on size.
  • Revisit your rate assumption whenever FTA announces regulatory changes.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the current VAT rate in the UAE?

The UAE's standard VAT rate is set by the Federal Tax Authority (FTA); confirm the current rate on FTA's official website before relying on it for invoicing or filing.

Are all goods and services subject to UAE VAT?

No, certain sectors such as specific healthcare, education, and financial services may be zero-rated or exempt — check FTA guidance for the current list.

Who is required to register for VAT in the UAE?

Businesses exceeding the FTA's mandatory registration threshold in taxable supplies must register for VAT; voluntary registration is available below that threshold.

How is VAT-inclusive price calculated?

This calculator multiplies your amount by the VAT rate to estimate the VAT charge, then adds that to your original amount to show the VAT-inclusive total.

Is VAT the same across all Gulf Cooperation Council countries?

No, while several GCC countries have implemented VAT under a common framework, rates and exemptions can differ by country — check the specific country's tax authority for exact figures.
