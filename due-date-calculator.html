What This Calculator Estimates

Due date calculators typically estimate an expected delivery date by adding a set number of weeks to the first day of the last menstrual period (Naegele's rule) or by calculating from a known conception date, though only a small percentage of babies are actually born exactly on their calculated due date. Because individual pregnancies vary and clinical ultrasound dating can refine estimates, this calculator provides a general estimate, not a precise medical prediction.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is a due date typically calculated?

The most common method (Naegele's rule) adds a set number of weeks to the first day of your last menstrual period, assuming a standard cycle length; conception-date-based methods offer an alternative calculation.

How often are babies born exactly on their due date?

Only a small percentage of babies are born on their exact calculated due date — most arrive within a window of a couple weeks before or after, which is considered entirely normal.

Does irregular cycle length affect due date accuracy?

Yes, calendar-based due date estimates assume a fairly standard cycle length, so irregular cycles can make the estimate less precise — clinical ultrasound dating is often used to refine estimates in these cases.

Is an ultrasound more accurate than a calculated due date?

Early ultrasound dating (particularly in the first trimester) is often considered more precise than calendar-based methods alone, especially for those with irregular cycles or uncertain last period dates.

Should I make medical decisions based solely on this estimate?

No, this provides a general estimate only — consult a healthcare provider for personalized prenatal care and more precise due date assessment.
