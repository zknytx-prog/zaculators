Percent Change Calculator

What This Calculator Estimates

Percent change is a general measure of how much a value has changed relative to its original amount, capturing both increases and decreases in a single calculation — a positive result indicates growth, while a negative result indicates decline. This calculator computes percent change using the original and new values you provide, useful when you're not sure in advance whether the change will be an increase or decrease.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter the original value.
  2. Enter the new value you want to compare.
  3. Click Calculate Percent Change.
  4. Review whether the result is an increase or decrease.
  5. Update the values to test another comparison.

Tips for a More Accurate Estimate

  • Use percent change when you're unsure in advance whether the result will be an increase or decrease.
  • Remember a negative result indicates a decrease, while a positive result indicates an increase.
  • Apply this to any numerical comparison, not just financial or business contexts.
  • Compare percent change (not just raw difference) when values being compared are very different in scale.
  • Double-check your original and new value inputs are entered in the correct order.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is percent change calculated?

Subtract the original value from the new value, divide by the original value, then multiply by 100 — a positive result means an increase, a negative result means a decrease.

How is percent change different from dedicated increase/decrease calculators?

Percent change handles both directions in one calculation, showing a positive or negative result depending on whether the value grew or shrank, while dedicated increase/decrease tools assume you already know the direction.

What does a negative percent change mean?

A negative result indicates the new value is lower than the original, representing a decline — the magnitude (ignoring the sign) tells you the percentage decrease.

Can percent change be used for any type of numerical comparison?

Yes, this calculation applies broadly — prices, scores, populations, measurements, or any numerical value being compared at two points in time or between two conditions.

Why might percent change be more useful than looking at raw difference alone?

Percent change standardizes the comparison relative to the starting value, which is often more meaningful than a raw difference, especially when comparing changes across values of very different magnitudes.
