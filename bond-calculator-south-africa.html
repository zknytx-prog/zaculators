Frequently Asked Questions

What is a 'bond' in South African property terminology?

In South Africa, 'bond' commonly refers to the home loan/mortgage secured against a property, and 'bond repayment' refers to the monthly mortgage payment — different terminology from some other English-speaking countries but a similar underlying concept.

Are South African bond rates typically fixed or variable?

Variable rates linked to the prime lending rate are common in South Africa, though some lenders also offer fixed-rate options for a portion of the loan term — check with specific banks for current offerings.

What is the prime lending rate and how does it affect my bond?

The prime lending rate is set based on South African Reserve Bank policy and serves as a benchmark that banks use to price variable-rate bonds, meaning your repayment can change if the prime rate changes.

What additional costs come with a South African bond beyond the loan itself?

Costs like bond registration fees, transfer duty (a property transfer tax), and attorney fees are typically required in addition to your deposit and should be budgeted for separately.

Can I pay off my South African bond early?

Many South African bonds allow additional payments or early settlement, which can significantly reduce total interest paid — check your specific loan agreement for any applicable terms or penalties.
