What This Calculator Estimates

BMI percentile, commonly used for children and teens, compares a young person's BMI to a reference population of others the same age and sex, expressed as a percentile rather than the fixed adult BMI categories, since healthy body composition changes significantly as children grow. Pediatric growth charts from health organizations are the standard reference for interpreting these percentiles, and this calculator provides a general estimate based on the values you provide.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does BMI percentile differ from adult BMI categories?
Children's bodies change significantly as they grow, so a fixed BMI number doesn't mean the same thing at every age — percentile compares a child's BMI to others of the same age and sex using standardized growth charts.

What percentile range is generally considered healthy?
Standard pediatric growth charts typically classify percentile ranges into underweight, healthy weight, overweight, and obese categories — check current CDC or WHO growth chart guidance for exact ranges.

Does this calculator use official growth chart data?
It provides a general estimate; for a clinically precise percentile, use official CDC or WHO growth chart tools or consult a pediatrician.

Should BMI percentile be tracked over time for children?
Yes, tracking percentile trends over time is often more informative than a single reading, since consistent growth along a percentile curve is generally a good sign.

Who should interpret a child's BMI percentile result?
A pediatrician or healthcare provider is best positioned to interpret BMI percentile in the context of a child's overall growth and health history.
