Frequently Asked Questions

What's the standard formula for converting salary to hourly rate?

The common baseline is annual salary ÷ 2,080 hours (40 hours/week × 52 weeks), but this should be adjusted if your actual work schedule differs from full-time standard hours.

Why would I want to know my hourly equivalent rate?

This is useful for comparing salaried job offers to hourly/contract offers, estimating overtime value, or understanding the value of your time for freelance or side work pricing.

Does this account for unpaid overtime salaried employees often work?

Not automatically — if you regularly work more hours than the standard assumption without additional pay, your true effective hourly rate is lower than this calculator shows using standard hours.

Should I use gross or net salary for this conversion?

Using gross salary gives your gross hourly equivalent; if you want net (after-tax) hourly rate, use your net annual salary instead, adjusted for tax already factored in.

How does part-time work affect this calculation?

If you work part-time, adjust the annual hours figure downward to reflect your actual scheduled hours rather than assuming a full 2,080-hour year.

This calculator provides hourly pay estimates only. Actual gross and net pay can vary by schedule, benefits, and local rules.
