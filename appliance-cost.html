Appliance Cost Calculator

What This Calculator Estimates

Total appliance cost includes not just the purchase price but also ongoing energy consumption costs over the appliance's useful life, meaning a cheaper upfront appliance isn't always the most cost-effective choice if it's significantly less energy-efficient than a pricier alternative. This calculator estimates total cost of ownership using the purchase price, energy consumption, and expected lifespan figures you provide.

Formula / Method Used

Daily kWh = (Watts × Hours Used) ÷ 1000. Monthly cost = Daily kWh × Rate × 30. Annual cost = Monthly cost × 12.

Worked Example

A 1,500-watt appliance run 1 hour per day at $0.15/kWh uses 1.5 kWh daily, costing about $6.75 per month and $81 per year.

How to Interpret the Result

Higher wattage and longer daily use both increase cost directly. Use the annual figure to compare appliances side by side, or to judge whether a more efficient replacement would pay for itself over time.

Common Mistakes

  • Using rated wattage when actual draw is lower (check the nameplate for "running" vs "peak" watts).
  • Forgetting to include standby power draw for always-on devices.
  • Mixing up watts and kilowatts.
  • Using an outdated electricity rate from an old bill.

Tips for a More Accurate Estimate

  • Compare total cost of ownership (purchase plus energy costs), not just upfront purchase price.
  • Check standardized energy efficiency labels when comparing appliance models before buying.
  • Factor in expected maintenance or repair costs for a more complete cost comparison.
  • Consider your actual expected usage frequency, since it significantly affects total energy cost.
  • Recalculate if energy rates or your usage patterns change significantly after purchase.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why should I consider total cost, not just purchase price, for appliances?

A less expensive appliance with poor energy efficiency can cost more over its lifetime than a pricier, more efficient model, once cumulative energy costs are factored in over years of use.

How do I estimate an appliance's expected energy cost over time?

Multiply its power consumption by typical usage hours and your electricity rate, then extend that estimate across the appliance's expected lifespan for a total energy cost projection.

Do energy-efficient appliances always cost more upfront?

Often yes, energy-efficient models can carry a price premium, though this is frequently offset by lower running costs over the appliance's lifetime, especially for appliances used frequently.

Should I consider maintenance and repair costs too?

Yes, for a truly complete total cost of ownership picture, expected maintenance or repair costs over the appliance's lifespan can also meaningfully affect the comparison between models.

How can I compare appliance energy efficiency before purchasing?

Look for standardized energy efficiency labels or ratings (common in many countries) that allow direct comparison of expected energy consumption across similar appliance models.

This calculator provides electricity cost estimates only. Actual utility rates, tiered pricing, and appliance efficiency can vary.
