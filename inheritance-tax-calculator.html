What This Calculator Estimates

Inheritance and estate taxes vary enormously across countries — some jurisdictions (like the UK) tax the estate before distribution above a threshold, others (like several US states) tax the inheritor directly, and many countries have no inheritance tax at all. Because rules, thresholds, and exemptions differ so significantly by jurisdiction and relationship to the deceased, this calculator provides a general estimate using the rate and threshold you enter for your specific situation.

Formula / Method Used

  1. Taxable estate = max(estate value - tax-free allowance, 0).
  2. Estimated inheritance tax = taxable estate x tax rate.
  3. The page does not automatically add gift rules, spousal exemptions, residence reliefs, or charitable reductions.

Worked Example

If the estate value is $800,000, the allowance is $325,000, and the tax rate is 40%, the taxable estate becomes $475,000. Applying the 40% rate gives an estimated inheritance tax bill of $190,000.

What the Result Means

The main result is the estimated tax bill based on the allowance and rate you supplied. The supporting line shows the amount treated as taxable after the allowance has been removed.

Common Mistakes

  1. Assuming every estate uses the same tax-free threshold.
  2. Ignoring lifetime gifts, spouse transfers, residence reliefs, or charitable exemptions.
  3. Using a general estimate in place of estate-specific legal or tax advice.

Tips for a More Accurate Estimate

  • Confirm whether your jurisdiction taxes the estate or the individual inheritor.
  • Check exemption thresholds based on your relationship to the deceased.
  • Understand whether any gifts made before death count toward the taxable estate.
  • Consult an estate planning professional for significant estates, given the complexity involved.
  • Recheck thresholds periodically, since many jurisdictions adjust them over time.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do all countries have inheritance tax?

No, many countries and jurisdictions have no inheritance or estate tax at all, while others apply it above certain thresholds — check your specific jurisdiction's rules.

Is inheritance tax paid by the estate or the inheritor?

This depends on the jurisdiction — some tax the estate itself before distribution (estate tax), while others tax the individual receiving the inheritance (inheritance tax) — the terminology and mechanics differ.

Are there exemptions based on relationship to the deceased?

Yes, many jurisdictions offer reduced rates or higher exemption thresholds for spouses, children, or other close relatives compared to more distant relations or unrelated beneficiaries.

Does this calculator apply my jurisdiction's exact rules automatically?

No, enter the threshold and rate applicable to your specific jurisdiction and relationship category for an accurate estimate.

Where can I confirm current official rules?

Your country's tax authority publishes current inheritance/estate tax thresholds, rates, and relationship-based exemptions — this is worth confirming with a legal or tax advisor given the complexity.

Limitations / Disclaimer

This calculator provides a simplified inheritance-tax estimate only and is not legal, estate, or tax advice. Estate planning can depend on asset type, relationship, timing of gifts, domicile, and current law.
