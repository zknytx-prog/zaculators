What This Calculator Estimates

Saudi Arabia does not levy personal income tax on salaries, so gross and net salary are largely the same for most residents aside from mandatory GOSI social insurance contributions, which apply differently to Saudi nationals versus expatriate employees. This calculator helps you model salary figures or convert between monthly and annual pay, keeping in mind that GOSI deductions (where applicable) are the main thing separating gross from net pay in most cases.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Saudi Arabia tax personal salary income?

No, Saudi Arabia does not impose personal income tax on employment salaries, which is a key difference from many other countries.

What deductions apply to a Saudi salary?

GOSI (General Organization for Social Insurance) contributions apply, with different rates for Saudi nationals (broader coverage including pension) versus non-Saudi employees (typically occupational hazards only).

Are there other mandatory deductions for expatriates in Saudi Arabia?

Generally no personal income tax or mandatory pension deductions for expatriates beyond limited GOSI coverage, though company-specific policies may vary.

Why is basic salary emphasized separately from allowances?

End-of-service benefits and GOSI contributions are typically calculated based on basic salary, so understanding your basic-to-allowance split matters for those calculations.

Is this useful for comparing job offers in Saudi Arabia?

Yes, since there's no income tax to factor in, comparing gross salary and benefits packages directly is relatively straightforward.
