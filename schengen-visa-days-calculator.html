Schengen Visa Days Calculator

What This Calculator Estimates

The Schengen Area's short-stay visa rule limits visitors to 90 days within any 180-day rolling period, calculated on a continuously moving window rather than a fixed calendar period — meaning you need to count backward from any given date to check compliance, not simply track calendar months. This calculator helps estimate your remaining allowed days based on your trip history, though official border authorities use the exact rolling 180-day calculation for enforcement.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the 90/180 day rule based on calendar months?

No, it's a rolling 180-day window — for any specific day, you look back exactly 180 days and count how many of those days you spent in the Schengen Area, which must not exceed 90.

Does the 90-day limit reset at the start of each calendar year?

No, since it's a rolling window rather than a fixed period, your allowance doesn't simply reset on January 1st — it continuously recalculates based on your trips in the trailing 180 days.

Does this rule apply to all Schengen countries the same way?

Yes, the 90/180 rule applies across the entire Schengen Area collectively for short-stay visits, not separately per country, since it's about total days within the combined area.

What happens if I overstay the 90-day limit?

Overstaying can result in fines, entry bans, or other immigration consequences depending on the country and circumstances — always track your days carefully if you travel frequently in the region.

Does this calculator account for long-stay visas or residence permits?

No, this addresses the short-stay 90/180 rule for visa-free or short-stay visa travel. Long-stay visas and residence permits follow entirely different rules specific to the issuing country.

This calculator provides Schengen travel-day estimates only. Official entry, visa, and rolling-period rules can change, so verify current official guidance before relying on the result.
