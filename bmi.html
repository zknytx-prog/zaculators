What This Calculator Estimates

Body Mass Index (BMI) is a simple screening measure that divides weight by the square of height to estimate whether someone falls into an underweight, normal, overweight, or obese category, widely used by health organizations as a quick population-level screening tool rather than a precise individual diagnostic measure. Because BMI doesn't distinguish between muscle and fat mass, it can misclassify very muscular or very lean individuals, so it works best as a starting point alongside other health indicators rather than a standalone verdict.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does BMI estimate?
BMI estimates body size relative to height and weight using a simple screening formula (weight divided by height squared), placing the result into standard categories like underweight, normal, overweight, or obese.

Is BMI a medical diagnosis?
No, BMI is an informational screening measure, not a medical diagnosis. Two people with the same BMI can have very different body compositions and health profiles.

Why can BMI be misleading for athletic people?
BMI doesn't distinguish between muscle and fat mass, so muscular individuals with low body fat can be classified as 'overweight' or 'obese' by BMI alone despite being fit and healthy.

What other measures complement BMI?
Waist-to-hip ratio, body fat percentage, and waist circumference are commonly used alongside BMI to get a fuller picture of body composition and health risk.

How often should I recalculate my BMI?
Recalculate whenever your weight changes meaningfully, and treat trends over time as more informative than any single BMI reading.
