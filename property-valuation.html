Frequently Asked Questions

What's the most common method for residential property valuation?

Comparable sales analysis (comparing to similar properties recently sold nearby) is the most commonly used method for residential properties, since it directly reflects what buyers are actually paying in the current market.

How does the income approach work for valuation?

This method values a property based on the income it generates (for rental properties), typically using a capitalization rate to convert net operating income into an estimated property value.

Is an online valuation estimate as accurate as a professional appraisal?

No, automated or simple estimates provide a general ballpark, while professional appraisals involve in-person inspection and detailed comparable analysis, making them significantly more reliable for actual transactions.

Why might different valuation methods give different results for the same property?

Each method captures different aspects of value (recent sales, income potential, rebuilding cost), so results can vary — professional appraisers often reconcile multiple methods to arrive at a final estimate.

When do I need an official property appraisal versus a general estimate?

Official appraisals are typically required for mortgage lending, legal disputes, and formal transactions, while general estimates are useful for informal planning and preliminary decision-making.
