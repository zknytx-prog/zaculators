What This Calculator Estimates

An equity loan (often a home equity loan) lets you borrow a lump sum against the equity you've built in an asset, typically repaid through fixed installments over an agreed term, similar in structure to a standard installment loan but usually secured by the underlying asset. This calculator estimates monthly payments and total interest using the loan amount, rate, and term you provide for your specific equity loan scenario.

Formula / Method Used

  • Estimated home equity = home value - current mortgage balance
  • Available loan = home value x max LTV - mortgage balance
  • Remaining equity after loan = home equity - available loan

If the available loan estimate is negative, this page shows zero borrowing room under the chosen LTV cap.

Worked Example

If a home is worth $425,000, the current mortgage balance is $240,000, and the target maximum LTV is 80%, the calculator estimates the total debt allowed at that LTV and subtracts the mortgage balance to estimate the additional borrowing room.

What the Result Means

Estimated home equity shows the value still owned after subtracting the mortgage balance. Maximum possible loan amount shows the borrowing room implied by the maximum LTV entered. Remaining equity after loan shows how much equity would still be left if the full estimated loan amount were used.

Common Mistakes

  • Assuming the result is an approval amount.
  • Using an outdated home value estimate instead of a current one.
  • Ignoring closing costs, lender fees, or minimum equity requirements.
  • Choosing an LTV assumption that does not match the product being considered.

Limitations / Disclaimer

This calculator provides informational estimates only and is not mortgage, legal, tax, or lending advice. Real equity loan limits depend on appraisals, credit, income, product rules, and lender underwriting. Results are estimates only.

Tips for a More Accurate Estimate

  • Compare equity loan rates against unsecured alternatives, since secured loans are often cheaper.
  • Remember your asset is at risk if you can't keep up with payments on a secured equity loan.
  • Consider a HELOC instead if you want flexible access rather than a lump sum.
  • Model different terms to balance monthly affordability against total interest cost.
  • Use funds for purposes that build long-term value, like renovations, when possible.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is an equity loan different from a line of credit?

An equity loan provides a lump sum with fixed repayment terms, while a line of credit (like a HELOC) offers flexible access to funds up to a limit, with interest charged only on the amount drawn — the right choice depends on how you plan to use the funds.

What can equity loans typically be used for?

Common uses include home renovations, debt consolidation, major purchases, or covering large expenses, though the specific use often affects whether it's the most cost-effective borrowing option compared to alternatives.

Are equity loan rates typically lower than unsecured loans?

Since equity loans are secured by the underlying asset, rates are often lower than unsecured personal loans, though this varies by lender and your specific credit profile.

What's the risk of an equity loan compared to unsecured borrowing?

Since equity loans are secured by an asset (often your home), failing to repay puts that asset at risk of foreclosure or repossession, unlike unsecured debt which carries different (though still serious) consequences for default.

How does loan term affect my equity loan payment?

A longer term lowers monthly payments but increases total interest paid over the life of the loan, similar to other installment loan structures.
