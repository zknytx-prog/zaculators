What This Calculator Estimates

Return on investment (ROI) for rental property is typically calculated by comparing your net profit (rental income minus all expenses and financing costs) against your total cash invested (down payment, closing costs, initial repairs), giving a percentage that helps compare this investment against other opportunities. Because ROI calculations can be structured differently (cash-on-cash return versus total return including appreciation), this calculator uses the inputs you provide to estimate a specific version of ROI for your scenario.

Tips for a More Accurate Estimate

  • Be clear on whether you're calculating cash-on-cash return or total ROI including appreciation.
  • Include only actual cash invested (down payment, closing costs, repairs) in your ROI base.
  • Compare ROI across different potential properties for consistent decision-making.
  • Weigh ROI alongside risk and liquidity, not as the sole factor in an investment decision.
  • Recalculate periodically as rental income, expenses, or property value change.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between cash-on-cash return and total ROI?

Cash-on-cash return focuses on annual cash flow relative to cash invested, while total ROI can also include property appreciation and equity buildup — the two can differ significantly, especially in appreciating markets.

What counts as 'cash invested' for this calculation?

Typically your down payment, closing costs, and any initial repair or renovation costs — not the full purchase price if you financed most of it with a mortgage.

How does financing affect ROI compared to buying in cash?

Using leverage (a mortgage) can increase percentage ROI on your actual cash invested compared to an all-cash purchase, though it also introduces financing costs and risk that an all-cash approach avoids.

Is a higher ROI always the better investment choice?

Not necessarily — ROI should be considered alongside risk, liquidity, time commitment, and how it fits your overall investment strategy, not viewed as the only factor in isolation.

Should I compare rental property ROI against other investment types?

Yes, comparing expected ROI (with appropriate risk adjustment) against other investment options like stocks or bonds can help you decide whether real estate fits your specific portfolio goals.
