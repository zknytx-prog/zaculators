What This Calculator Estimates

Canadian income tax combines federal tax (administered by the CRA using progressive bands) with provincial or territorial tax, which has its own separate bands and rates depending on where you live, meaning your total combined rate varies significantly by province. Because federal and provincial taxes stack together with different thresholds, this calculator uses an editable rate so you can model your specific combined bracket.

Formula / Method Used

  1. The page applies 15% to the first federal band used in the calculator and 20.5% to income above that band.
  2. It then multiplies your full income by the provincial rate you enter.
  3. Total estimated tax = estimated federal tax + estimated provincial tax.
  4. Estimated after-tax income = annual income - total estimated tax.

Worked Example

Using annual income of C$70,000 and an entered provincial rate of 10%, the calculator estimates about C$11,277.32 in federal tax and C$7,000 in provincial tax. Total estimated tax is C$18,277.32, leaving about C$51,722.68 after the estimated income tax shown here.

What the Result Means

The main figure is the combined federal and entered provincial tax estimate. The supporting text shows the approximate after-tax income left after only those two layers. It is not a full payslip or return calculation.

Common Mistakes

  1. Entering a provincial rate that does not match your province or territory.
  2. Assuming the result includes CPP, EI, credits, deductions, and every local rule automatically.
  3. Comparing a yearly estimate to a monthly payslip without converting periods.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I pay both federal and provincial income tax in Canada?

Yes, Canadians pay federal income tax to the CRA plus provincial or territorial income tax, with each level having its own separate bands and rates.

Which province has the highest combined tax rate?

Combined federal-provincial top marginal rates vary by province and change periodically — check current CRA and provincial tax tables for an accurate comparison.

Does this calculator combine federal and provincial tax automatically?

No, this estimates income tax generally; pair it with the Federal Income Tax Calculator specifically, plus your province's current tax tables, for a full combined estimate.

What tax credits can reduce my Canadian income tax?

Common credits include the basic personal amount, CPP/EI contribution credits, and various province-specific credits — check CRA guidance for what applies to your situation.

Where can I confirm current official federal and provincial rates?

The Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) publishes federal rates, while each province's ministry of finance publishes its own provincial rate tables.

Limitations / Disclaimer
