Frequently Asked Questions

What is nisab in the context of zakat?

Nisab is the minimum threshold of wealth (traditionally defined in terms of gold or silver value) that must be held before zakat becomes obligatory — wealth below this threshold is generally not subject to zakat.

Does zakat apply to all types of wealth the same way?

No, different asset types (cash savings, gold and silver, business inventory, investments, agricultural produce) can have different specific rules and sometimes different rates according to various schools of Islamic jurisprudence.

How long must wealth be held before zakat is due?

Generally, wealth must be held above the nisab threshold for a full lunar year (Hawl) before zakat becomes due on it, though specific rules can vary by asset type and scholarly interpretation.

What is the standard zakat rate?

A commonly referenced rate for many types of wealth is 2.5%, though this can vary by asset category and school of thought — check with a knowledgeable source for the rate applicable to your specific assets.

Should I consult a religious scholar for my specific zakat calculation?

Yes, given the nuances across different asset types, schools of thought, and personal circumstances, consulting a knowledgeable imam or Islamic scholar is recommended for precise guidance beyond this general estimate.
